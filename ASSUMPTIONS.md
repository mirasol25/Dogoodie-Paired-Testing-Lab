# Preliminary Product and Methodology Assumptions

This document records assumptions made solely to create a presentable and modifiable front-end preparation prototype. It is not a protocol approval, expert opinion, legal conclusion, scientific validation, or forensic specification.

**All assumptions require confirmation from the intended expert witness and qualified legal, statistical, and forensic reviewers.**

## Study design assumptions

- The prototype compares prices displayed by the same fictional platform to different synthetic account profiles.
- The illustrative geography is New York City.
- The route is a Midtown Manhattan hotel corridor to JFK Airport Terminal 4.
- Membership status is the primary isolated variable.
- The same platform, pickup, destination, ride tier, currency, and study window should be held fixed.
- Operating-system family and application version should match.
- Network category may become a required matching control.
- The illustrative target is 100 technically usable pairs.
- The testing period is 14 days.

## Threshold assumptions

- A timestamp difference of five seconds or less passes.
- A timestamp difference above five and through ten seconds produces a warning.
- A timestamp difference above ten seconds fails.
- A GPS distance of five feet or less passes.
- A GPS distance above five and through fifteen feet produces a warning.
- A GPS distance above fifteen feet fails.
- Thresholds are preliminary demonstrations, not statistically, scientifically, or legally validated values.

## Evidence assumptions

- A quote screenshot is required for each tester.
- A full-screen-recording placeholder is required for each tester.
- Device, operating system, application version, network, timestamp, coordinates, route, tier, assignment, tester, and notes metadata are useful.
- Synthetic evidence hashes are display labels only and are not cryptographic attestations.
- A demonstration activity log is useful, but it is not immutable or tamper-proof.
- The proposed evidence-package structure is suitable only for preparation and requires expert and counsel review.

## Workflow and user assumptions

- Four demonstration roles are useful: Test Coordinator, Tester, Expert Reviewer, and Law-Firm Viewer.
- Expert review includes accept, flag, reject, clear, reason, note, and follow-up concepts.
- A local tester countdown is a useful simulation of synchronized requests.
- A reviewer decision should update pair lists, metrics, events, and reports immediately.
- A reset action should restore deterministic fixtures.
- Role selection and demonstration state may be persisted locally in the browser.
- Presentation-level role restrictions are adequate for this prototype but are not production authorization.

## Analysis and report assumptions

- Descriptive analytics only are appropriate before expert methodology is received.
- Counts, percentages, mean, median, minimum, maximum, distributions, observed variance, validation rate, and review rate are acceptable demonstration metrics.
- P-values, confidence intervals, statistical significance, causal conclusions, discrimination conclusions, and liability conclusions must not be displayed.
- US Letter is the default report format.
- A4 support may be enabled through print configuration.
- CSV, JSON, and browser-printable outputs are useful demonstration formats.
- The proposed report inventory and terminology require legal and expert review.

## Product and technical assumptions

- A front-end-only Next.js application is sufficient for the preparation MVP.
- Deterministic synthetic fixtures are preferable to a database or external services.
- Zustand local persistence is sufficient for demonstration continuity.
- No real authentication, uploads, cloud storage, external APIs, rideshare integration, analytics, or location collection is required.
- Production security, privacy, evidence integrity, retention, access control, and audit requirements remain undefined.

## Required follow-up

The next discovery process should validate:

- Exact comparison focus and alternative explanations
- Existing manual testing and coordination workflow
- Required controls, tolerances, and exclusion rules
- Required evidence, provenance, and forensic handling
- Tester readiness and synchronization procedures
- User permissions and separation of duties
- Reviewer decisions, escalation, and correction workflows
- Export, manifest, exhibit, and downstream statistical formats
- Approved legal and methodological terminology
- Statistical design, sampling, power, analysis, and limitations
- Production authentication, authorization, encryption, storage, retention, logging, and incident response
